Homworks Interior Guide

12 things to settle before the work begins.

A short checklist from the Homworks Interior Guide — the details that save the most rework later.

  1. Decide your furniture layout before you finalise plans for false ceilings, lighting, wall electricals, plumbing and gas pipelines — it avoids obstructed loft openings or wall damage during installation later on.

  2. Decide on your finishes based on your existing flooring, so the design you dreamt of is the design you get.

  3. Choose your finishes before your paint colour. It gives a cohesive, pleasing look and makes sure the paint complements the design.

  4. Tell your designer about the furniture you already own, so it can be integrated seamlessly — optimising design and function without compromise.

  5. Make sure your designer knows the colours of your internal doors and windows, so they blend into your interior palette.

  6. Plan cable organisation as part of the installation, to prevent a chaotic network of cables — a tidy, clutter-free and safer space.

  7. Decide the TV model and size in advance. It sets the back-panel size, keeps wire management tidy and gives you the right viewing distance.

  8. Ask your designer to explain the difference between cot size and mattress size — the terms vary from person to person. It avoids confusion in space planning and wall electrical points, and shows whether customisation is possible at this stage.

  9. Consider the natural light entering each room before you choose finishes. Darker colours in a poorly lit space can feel cramped and push up daytime electricity use.

  10. Tell your designer about the kitchen appliances you already have, to prevent service, access and safety issues — even the vent provisions can then be designed around them.

  11. A barrier-free environment allows free, safe movement, function and access for everyone, regardless of age, gender or condition. It’s the designer’s duty to ensure this before any other aspect of design.

  12. Set a realistic budget with your designer. Whether you are renovating a house or designing a new building, an accurate, well-thought-out budget is essential to the success of the project.

Finishes

A spectrum of texture and colour.

Texture and colour are two fundamental components of interior design. They evoke a wide range of emotions and moods, create contrast and harmony, and add depth and dimension.

Texture is the tactile quality of a surface — smooth or rough, soft or hard, matte or glossy. Colour runs from warm and vibrant to cool and calming, and can evoke very different feelings in the people who live with it.

Finish 01

Lamination

The most common finish, and it withstands any rough surface. It brings out a natural, seamless look.

Finish 02

Membrane

A unique finish with a natural appeal, at home in traditional and contemporary designs — in matte, gloss, wooden and many colours.

Finish 03

Acrylic

A glassy, almost mirror-like surface with a plush sheen — non-toxic, and available in a wide range of colours.

Finish 04

PU paint

A finish that elevates the space, protects the cabinets and lasts longer — sturdier and stronger.
